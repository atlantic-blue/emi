# The contracts version 1 must satisfy

A contract says what one part of Emi must do. It states the input, the output and every error that
part must produce. A contract with no stated error is an incomplete contract.

Each contract carries an identifier, for example `TOKEN-1`. The feature that builds each one is
named in `features.md`, and a test reads the two documents together. A contract in one document and
absent from the other fails the pipeline.

## How to read this document

Each contract says how it is verified.

A contract verified by a test is proved by a green run in the pipeline. A contract verified by the
operator is one no test can judge, such as whether a logo is good, so the operator looks at a
rendered result before the step closes. A few contracts are verified both ways.

The contracts are grouped. There are twelve groups.

`TOKEN` is a design token and its measured value. `BRAND` is a drawn asset. `SEE` is accessibility.
`TABLE` is one table, at field level. `ENVELOPE` is the encrypted record format. `VAULT` is key
handling on the phone. `CYCLE` is the prediction arithmetic. `SCREEN` is what one screen does.
`WIRE` is one endpoint of the application programming interface. `AUTH` is the request signature.
`PAY` is the subscription. `KEEP` is a promise about her data.

This document does not say what is built today. `architecture.md` says that, section by section.

## TOKEN, the design tokens

### TOKEN-1, the colour set

Verified by a test.

Input: none. The tokens are constants.

Output: eighteen named colours, with the values the design fixed.

Errors: a hex value written anywhere outside `packages/tokens` fails the lint rule and the build.

### TOKEN-2, contrast is enforced and not documented

Verified by a test.

Input: every token marked as a text colour, and the ground it is used on.

Output: a contrast ratio for each pair.

Errors: a ratio below 4.5 fails the test, and the failure names the token and the ratio. A text
token that names no ground fails, because an unmeasured colour is the thing this contract exists to
stop.

### TOKEN-3, the type scale

Verified by a test.

Input: none.

Output: six sizes, each with its line height and its face.

Errors: a size whose line height is below 1.2 times the size.

## BRAND, the drawn assets

### BRAND-1, the wordmark

Verified by the operator, who looks at the rendered mark.

Output: `emi` in lowercase Fraunces. The dot of the `i` is a ring of the stem's stroke width, with a
gap of 40 degrees that opens at the upper left.

Errors: the gap is not visible when the mark is drawn at 16 points. The step widens the gap until it
is visible, and it records the final angle.

### BRAND-2, the application icon

Verified by a test for the sizes, and by the operator for the drawing.

Input: `brand/logo/emi-ring.svg`.

Output: every icon size both stores require, generated by one TypeScript program.

Errors: a size the store asks for is missing. The ring is not at 56 percent of the width. The source
is a shell script rather than TypeScript.

### BRAND-3, the ring geometry

Verified by a test.

Input: a cycle of some number of days, the current day, and the four phase boundaries.

Output: four arcs sized by their own day counts, a gap of 3 degrees of ground between neighbouring
arcs, elapsed days at full strength, days ahead at 20 percent, and a bead on the current day.

Errors: the arcs and the gaps do not total 360 degrees. A gap is absent. The bead sits on a day
outside the cycle. A phase of zero days draws an arc rather than nothing.

### BRAND-4, the illustration refuses five subjects

Verified by the operator, who looks at each piece.

Output: abstract shapes only.

Errors: a body, a face, a flower, a droplet or blood appears in any illustration.

## SEE, accessibility

### SEE-1, the ring is readable without colour

Verified by a test.

Input: the rendered ring.

Output: a gap in the ground carries every phase boundary, and the current phase is named in words
inside the ring.

Errors: a boundary that exists only as a change of colour. A phase name that is absent.

The measurement behind this contract: simulated for deuteranopia, the period and follicular colours
both become olive, and the ovulation and luteal colours both become slate. A ring that separates its
phases by colour alone is unreadable to about one man in twelve. This contract may not be weakened.

### SEE-2, a phase fill never carries text

Verified by a test.

Output: text sits on the darker ink partner of a phase colour, never on the fill.

Errors: text of any size is drawn on the period, follicular, ovulation or luteal fill.

### SEE-3, tap targets

Verified by a test.

Output: every interactive element is at least 44 points on both axes, except a day square of a
calendar month. A day square is at least 44 points high. Its touch is the width of its column plus
half the gap on each side, so no point in the row belongs to no square.

Errors: any other element below 44 points, named with its measured size. A day square whose touch
leaves a point of its row with no square.

### SEE-4, motion respects the setting

Verified by a test.

Output: the ring animates once on open, and it stays still when the operating system asks for
reduced motion.

Errors: the ring animates while the reduced motion setting is on.

## TABLE, the tables at field level

### TABLE-1, the day log table

Verified by a test.

Output: the eight columns of the design, with their types, their nullability, and the unique
constraint on the day.

Errors: a row whose day is not written as a year, a month and a day. A second row with the same day.
A revision that does not rise on a write. A payload that is not a valid envelope. A write that
leaves the update time behind the creation time.

### TABLE-2, the cycle table is a cache and never a source

Verified by a test.

Output: the table is rebuilt from the day log whenever a period day changes.

Errors: a value in the cycle table that cannot be derived from the day log. Any attempt to sync this
table.

### TABLE-3, the sync state holds exactly one row

Verified by a test.

Output: one row, holding the account identifier, the cursor and the time of the last sync.

Errors: a second row is accepted.

### TABLE-4, the vault table

Verified by a test.

Output: the account item and the record item of the design, with the index on the update time.

Errors: an item carrying a date, a symptom, a flow or a note in any attribute. The whole product
rests on this one, so a test reads every attribute of a written item to prove it.

### TABLE-5, the profile table

Verified by a test.

Output: one table that holds at most one row, with seven columns: the identifier, the column that
holds the table to a single row, the sealed payload, the revision, the creation time, the update
time, and the revision the server has confirmed. The payload is one sealed profile record.

Errors: a second row. A revision that does not rise on a write. A revision below 1. An empty
payload. A creation time or an update time that is not written as an instant. An update time behind
the creation time. A confirmed revision above the revision. Any field of the profile written in
plain text, in this table or in any other. A row left behind after delete everything.

The record inside the payload carries the kind, the name, the year of birth, the cycle length in
days, the period length in days, the regularity, the feeling, the goals, the focus, and the time of
the write. The kind and the time of the write are required. Every other field is optional, and an
absent field is an answer she skipped. `ENVELOPE-2` refuses a value outside its range.

## ENVELOPE, the encrypted record format

### ENVELOPE-1, the format

Verified by a test.

Input: a plaintext record and a key of 32 bytes.

Output: one version byte of `0x01`, then a nonce of 24 bytes, then the ciphertext and its
authentication tag of 16 bytes.

Errors: a nonce used twice. A version byte that is not `0x01`. A truncated input. A flipped bit
anywhere, which the reader must refuse rather than decrypt.

### ENVELOPE-2, the plaintext shape

Verified by a test.

Input: the fields of the design, as canonical JSON with sorted keys and no whitespace. Two records
travel in this shape: the day record, and the profile record the first run writes. A profile record
names its kind inside the ciphertext and a day record names none, so a reader tells the two apart
after it opens them and the server cannot tell them apart at all.

Output: the same bytes every time, for the same record.

Errors: a key the shape does not name, in either record. In a day record, a temperature outside
34.0 to 42.0 degrees Celsius. A weight outside 20.0 to 400.0 kilograms. An energy outside 1 to 5. A
note longer than 2000 characters. A symptom slug that is not in the catalogue. A missing day. In a
profile record, a name longer than 40 characters once the spaces at each end are taken off, or a
name carrying a line break. A year of birth outside 1940 to the current year less nine. A cycle
length outside 21 to 45 days. A period length outside 1 to 15 days. A regularity, a feeling, a goal
or a focus that the design does not list. The same goal or the same focus twice. A missing kind. A
missing time of the write.

### ENVELOPE-3, fixed vectors

Verified by a test.

Input: a key, a nonce and a plaintext, all checked in.

Output: a checked in ciphertext, byte for byte.

Errors: a change to the format that the vectors do not catch. A new version byte must add vectors
rather than change them.

### ENVELOPE-4, the service cannot decrypt

Verified by a test.

Output: the vault service imports the envelope's validator and nothing else from the crypto package.

Errors: the service imports a decrypt function, or holds a key of any kind. A test reads the
service's own import graph.

## VAULT, key handling on the phone

### VAULT-1, the vault key

Verified by a test, and by the operator on a real device.

Input: none, at first run.

Output: 32 random bytes in the keychain, made once and never replaced.

Errors: a second creation while one exists. The key in any log, any export, any error message or any
crash report.

The operator's half: the key survives the application being deleted and installed again. That is the
platform's behaviour, and platforms change it, so it is measured on a device and not assumed.

### VAULT-2, the recovery code

Verified by a test.

Input: 26 characters of Crockford base 32, and a salt of 16 bytes.

Output: a key from Argon2id, and the vault key wrapped under it.

Errors: the code stored anywhere. The salt used by two accounts. An unlock slower than one second on
the slowest device tested, which moves the parameters and records the new number.

### VAULT-3, the lock

Verified by a test.

Output: the application asks for the device biometric on return from the background. The setting is
on by default.

Errors: any screen content is visible in the application switcher preview.

## CYCLE, the prediction arithmetic

### CYCLE-1, a cycle

Verified by a test.

Input: the day logs.

Output: a cycle starts on the first bleeding day that she did not mark unexpected, and it ends the
day before the next one.

Errors: a day she marked unexpected starts a cycle. A cycle of zero days. Two cycles that overlap.

### CYCLE-2, the forecast

Verified by a test.

Input: at least two complete cycles.

Output: a predicted start, as a range, from the median of the last six lengths and their spread.

Errors: a single date rather than a range, anywhere in the interface. A forecast from fewer than two
cycles. The mean used in place of the median.

### CYCLE-3, the learning state

Verified by a test.

Input: fewer than two complete cycles.

Output: the home screen says that Emi is still learning, and it uses the length she gave at first
run.

Errors: a confidence shown while Emi is learning.

### CYCLE-4, ovulation and the fertile window

Verified by a test.

Input: the predicted start, and a luteal length of 13 days until her temperature refines it.

Output: an estimated ovulation day, and a window from five days before it to one day after it.

Errors: any wording that a day is safe, or that a pregnancy can be prevented or achieved. A test
reads every string in the application for the forbidden wording.

### CYCLE-5, confidence bands are measured and not felt

Verified by a test.

Input: published distributions of cycle length, cited in the code.

Output: three band edges, each one traceable to its citation.

Errors: a band edge with no citation. Until this contract holds, the interface marks the bands
provisional.

## SCREEN, what one screen does

### SCREEN-1, first run

Verified by a test.

Output: at most eleven questions and five other screens between the welcome and the home screen.
Only the last period is required. It ends with her answers written in one transaction at the hold.

Errors: a question whose answer nothing in the product reads, except the year of birth. A question
with no Skip, other than the last period. An answer written to the database or the server in plain
text. An account, an email address or a password asked for. Anything written before the hold.

### SCREEN-2, the home screen

Verified by a test, and by the operator, who looks at the rendered screen.

Output: the ring, the cycle day, the phase name and the forecast range.

Errors: the words period, bleeding, fertile or ovulation drawn larger than 14 points. She opens this
screen in public, and a stranger at arm's length must read nothing.

### SCREEN-3, the log sheet

Verified by a test.

Output: flow, mood, energy, symptoms, temperature, weight and a note, in one sheet, saved in one
action.

Errors: a save that takes more than one action. A symptom that a search cannot find.

### SCREEN-4, the day view

Verified by a test.

Output: a past day, opened, read and edited, with the ring redrawn.

Errors: an edit that does not raise the revision. An edit to a future day.

## WIRE, the endpoints

### WIRE-1, register

Verified by a test.

Input: a public key, a wrapped vault key and a recovery salt, signed by the key being registered.

Output: an account identifier.

Errors: a public key that is already registered. A signature that does not verify. A body over 4
kilobytes.

### WIRE-2, put a record

Verified by a test.

Input: a record identifier, a revision and an envelope.

Output: the stored revision.

Errors: a revision that is not greater than the stored one, which the server refuses rather than
merges. An envelope over 64 kilobytes. An account with no active subscription, refused with a status
the application reads as pay and not as an error.

### WIRE-3, pull records

Verified by a test.

Input: a cursor.

Output: every record written after the cursor, and a new cursor.

Errors: a cursor from another account. A page over 1 megabyte, which returns a cursor instead of
truncating in silence.

### WIRE-4, delete everything

Verified by a test.

Input: nothing beyond the signature.

Output: every record item and the account item removed.

Errors: an item that remains, proved by a read after the call. A delay before the deletion. A
confirmation step on the server, because the confirmation belongs on the phone.

### WIRE-5, reads survive a lapse

Verified by a test.

Output: a pull and a delete work whatever the subscription says.

Errors: a read refused because a subscription lapsed.

## AUTH, the request signature

### AUTH-1, the signature

Verified by a test.

Input: the method, the path, an instant and the SHA-256 hash of the body, joined by newlines.

Output: an Ed25519 signature, verified against the stored public key.

Errors: an instant more than 300 seconds from now. A signature played back a second time. A body
whose hash does not match. A missing header. An account that does not exist, refused with the same
message and the same timing as a bad signature.

## PAY, the subscription

### PAY-1, the products

Verified by a test.

Output: one yearly product at 29.99 pounds, with a one month introductory free period, in both
stores.

Errors: a configuration test finds a different price, a different period, or no product at all.

### PAY-2, the entitlement

Verified by a test.

Input: the store, on launch.

Output: whether she is subscribed.

Errors: a store that cannot be reached costs her access, rather than the last known answer being
kept. A second install asks her to pay again when the store says she owns it.

### PAY-3, the receipt is checked on the server

Verified by a test.

Input: a store transaction.

Output: the date the subscription runs to, on the account item.

Errors: a receipt accepted with no call to Apple or to Google. A receipt from one store checked
against the other.

### PAY-4, a lapse is humane

Verified by a test.

Output: reading, exporting and deleting stay open forever. Only new writes stop.

Errors: anything that makes her own history unreadable to her.

## KEEP, the promises about her data

### KEEP-1, nothing about her reaches a log

Verified by a test.

Output: a log line carries a request identifier and a duration.

Errors: a payload, an account identifier, a signature, a public key or a store transaction in any
log line. A test drives every endpoint and reads the captured output.

### KEEP-2, export

Verified by a test.

Output: every record she has, as a file a machine can read and as a document she can read.

Errors: a field present in the database and absent from the export.

### KEEP-3, delete everything, in one action

Verified by a test.

Output: the local database empty, the keychain items gone, and the server account gone.

Errors: any row, any keychain item or any server item that remains. A way back after it. A delay or
a cooling off period.

### KEEP-4, no third party sees anything

Verified by a test.

Output: the application's dependency list holds no analytics, no advertising identifier and no crash
reporter that ships her content.

Errors: any such package is present, named by the test.
